# Introduction to Quantum Mechanics

## Overview

Quantum mechanics is a fundamental theory in physics that provides a description of the physical properties of nature at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles. It is the foundation of all quantum physics including quantum chemistry, quantum field theory, quantum technology, and quantum information science.

## Key Principles

### 1. Wave-Particle Duality

Particles exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties. This is famously demonstrated by the double-slit experiment, where electrons create an interference pattern when not observed, but behave as particles when measured.

### 2. The Schrödinger Equation

The fundamental equation of quantum mechanics:

```
iℏ ∂/∂t |Ψ(t)⟩ = Ĥ |Ψ(t)⟩
```

Where:
- ℏ is the reduced Planck constant
- |Ψ(t)⟩ is the quantum state vector
- Ĥ is the Hamiltonian operator

### 3. Uncertainty Principle

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle states that certain pairs of complementary variables cannot both be known with arbitrary precision:

```
Δx · Δp ≥ ℏ/2
```

### 4. Quantum Superposition

A quantum system can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. A qubit, for example, can be in a superposition of |0⟩ and |1⟩:

```
|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩
```

where |α|² + |β|² = 1.

## Applications

- **Quantum Computing**: Leveraging superposition and entanglement for computation
- **Quantum Cryptography**: Secure communication using quantum key distribution
- **Quantum Sensors**: Ultra-precise measurements using quantum effects
- **Semiconductor Physics**: Understanding electron behavior in materials
