# Principles of Evolutionary Biology

## Overview

Evolution is the central organizing principle of biology, describing how populations of organisms change over generations through mechanisms such as natural selection, genetic drift, and gene flow.

## Core Mechanisms

### 1. Natural Selection

Organisms with traits better suited to their environment tend to survive and reproduce more:

- **Directional Selection**: Favors one extreme phenotype
- **Stabilizing Selection**: Favors intermediate phenotypes
- **Disruptive Selection**: Favors both extremes

### 2. Genetic Drift

Random changes in allele frequencies, especially significant in small populations:

- **Bottleneck Effect**: Population crash reduces genetic diversity
- **Founder Effect**: New population established by few individuals

### 3. Gene Flow

Transfer of genetic material between populations through migration

### 4. Mutation

The ultimate source of genetic variation, providing raw material for evolution

## Evidence for Evolution

- **Fossil Record**: Transitional forms show gradual change over time
- **Comparative Anatomy**: Homologous structures indicate common ancestry
- **Molecular Biology**: DNA sequence comparisons reveal evolutionary relationships
- **Direct Observation**: Antibiotic resistance in bacteria, beak changes in finches

## Key Concepts

### Speciation

The formation of new species:
- **Allopatric**: Geographic separation leads to divergence
- **Sympatric**: Speciation without geographic isolation

### Phylogenetics

Reconstructing evolutionary relationships using:
- Morphological characteristics
- Molecular sequences
- Computational analysis
